# Self-serve, product-led AI tools by solo/small founders (2025-2026): revenue evidence, channels, failure patterns, and payment rails for a Bangladesh resident

**Method / source-quality note (read first):** Direct page fetches failed in this environment. trustmrr.com, indiehackers.com, dev.to, extensionpay.com, docs.polar.sh, agentaos.ai and getpaidatlas.com all returned DNS "host not allowed" errors. Every finding below comes from WebSearch result summaries and snippets, not from full page reads. Almost all revenue figures are **founder self-reported** or come from **secondary aggregators** such as startupfounderstories, stealwhatworks, mrrstory and Medium. TrustMRR numbers are Stripe-connected, so they are somewhat stronger, but TrustMRR is run by Marc Lou, who also runs other products, and buyers still do their own diligence. Treat every MRR figure as directional. Search date: 2026-11-22.

## Q1. Indie AI products with verified or self-reported MRR: category, channel, price, team size

### Takeaway
I found about 22 concrete examples. The solo or tiny-team AI products with the strongest evidence of real recurring revenue cluster in four places:
- **Browser extensions that sit inside an existing workflow:** ChatGPT, X/Twitter, Amazon Seller, exam and homework sites.
- **Consumer "AI output" products** sold on SEO and social: headshots, photos, ebooks.
- **"Engineering as marketing" free-tool funnels** feeding a B2B SaaS: SiteGPT, ChatSEO.
- **Shopify apps.**

The distribution is extremely long-tailed. In a sample of 5,596 TrustMRR startups, the median solo startup makes about $149 per 30 days, and only 5.5% reach $10k or more.

**Non-AI reference points for extension economics:**
- GMass, a Gmail mail-merge extension, reportedly makes ~$130K/month from ~10,000 subscribers at $8–20/month — [stealwhatworks / Chrome Goldmine](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/chrome-extensions-over-10k-month-now).

**Aggregate base rates:**
- In a TrustMRR sample of 5,596 startups, one-person teams had a median of $149 per 30 days. 24% reached $1k or more and 5.5% reached $10k or more. 56% were "rising", with 88% margins — [DEV analysis](https://dev.to/rudratosh/i-pulled-verified-revenue-for-5596-startups-heres-exactly-what-to-build-in-2026-11-benchmarks-4eij).
- AI is the largest TrustMRR category, at roughly 24% of startups and also roughly a quarter of the top 200 — [webscraperapi](https://www.webscraperapi.ai/blog/i-used-openclaw-to-analyze-trustmrrs-top-200-startups); [Crawlora](https://crawlora.net/datasets/trustmrr).
- One snapshot puts the median MRR of AI startups at about $177 — [mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/top-solo-founder-startups/).
- The TrustMRR sample over-represents very early, stagnant and community-driven projects, and skews towards Marc Lou's French/European audience — [onemilliongoal](https://www.onemilliongoal.com/p/what-986-solo-startups-actually-look).

### Inferences
- The products that grew via a store or SEO with no sales calls are mostly **B2C or prosumer at $5–40/month**: students, X creators, ChatGPT power users, Amazon sellers. B2B examples like SiteGPT and Chatbase still self-serve via SEO, but at higher ARPU.
- **Time to first meaningful revenue** is short for the winners, roughly 1–6 months: SiteGPT made $10K in month 1, ChatSEO reached €14K in 5 months, Easy Folders reached $3.7K in 6 months, and SuperX reached ~$10K in 6 months. But this is survivorship-biased. The median solo product never gets past ~$150/month.
- Many of the biggest "solo" AI winners (Levels, Dinh, Postma, Hallam) had a **pre-existing audience on X**. So "no sales calls" does not mean "no marketing". Audience or content was usually the real engine, and the store or SEO was the conversion surface.

### Gaps
- I could not open TrustMRR pages to verify current figures, price points or founding dates.
- Price points are missing for most examples.
- I found no reliable revenue data for Glasp, Tactiq, HARPA or Monica.
- Team size is often unstated.
- Profit AI and the Estha calculator case are weakly sourced and may be marketing content.

## Q2. Which channels show up most among the successes?

### Takeaway
Among the examples gathered, the channels show up in this order:
1. **SEO / free-tool funnels:** SiteGPT, ChatSEO, Neel Seth, Rezi, Photo AI, HeadshotPro, the AI calculator.
2. **Chrome Web Store extensions:** CheatMate, SuperX, Launch Fast, Superpower ChatGPT, Easy Folders.
3. **Founder audience on X:** SuperX, Photo AI, TypingMind.
4. **Shopify App Store:** Profit AI, Lorik Morina, Kaching.

Product Hunt and AI directories such as TAAFT and Futurepedia appear only as **supporting** channels. I found no documented case where they were the primary revenue driver.

### Cited Findings
- **Free tools as SEO:** SiteGPT's 50+ free tools drive ~90% of its Google traffic, and new tools are cloned from templates with AI coding assistants, each targeting a new keyword — [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/sitegpt). ChatSEO ships 2–4 free mini-tools a week as funnels — [mrrstory](https://www.mrrstory.com/stories/how-chatseo-grew-to-14k-mrr-using-only-organic-traffic).
- **Chrome extensions:** Typical freemium conversion is about 2–5% (one guide says often under 3%). Getting 200–500 paying users gives roughly $3K–10K MRR, but these are guide estimates, not data — [ExtensionPay/aggregator summary](https://extensionpay.com/articles/monetize-chrome-extensions-with-extensionpay); [Kromio](https://kromio.ai/learn/monetize-chrome-extension). The TimeYourWeb case (20K users, $60 MRR) shows installs do not equal revenue — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-a-chrome-extension-in-2-weeks-10-years-later-20-000-users-10-paying-customers-60-mrr-YaK5THoaKqXgTlgBjHgO).
- **Shopify:** Documented solo and 2-person apps concentrate in the $2K–20K MRR band, typically after 12–24 months of compounding (vendor source) — [adsx](https://adsx.com/blog/how-much-shopify-app-developers-make). Shopify charges developers a 0% revenue share on the first $1M of lifetime gross revenue from Jan 1, 2025, then 15% (not verified against Shopify docs) — same source.
- **Product Hunt:** Results range from zero to hundreds of paying customers. One founder got ~30K visitors and an estimated ~10 paying customers. Typical conversion is 0.3–1.5%, and below 0.5% it should be treated as branding — [DEV / startupcafe](https://dev.to/startupcafe/product-hunt-launches-look-impressive-but-do-they-bring-paying-customers-239l); [muroanalytics](https://muroanalytics.com/blog/which-traffic-source-converts-best).
- **TAAFT:** Claimed 1M+ monthly visitors. Traffic is "comparison-shaped", and task pages compound for months. Approval takes 3–7 days. I found **no TAAFT conversion or revenue data** for indie founders — [bestlaunchplatforms](https://bestlaunchplatforms.com/compare/product-hunt-vs-taaft); [0102.ai](https://www.0102.ai/blog/product-hunt-alternatives-2026).
- **X audience:** ~95% of SuperX users came from the founder's organic X content — [startupfounderstories](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/rob-hallam-superx-23k-mrr-x-content).
- **Newsletters bundled with an extension:** Superpower ChatGPT monetises through newsletter sponsorships plus paid software — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/building-a-free-chrome-extension-in-3-days-and-turning-it-into-a-5-figure-mrr-ecosystem-3rIbjigZxiFsrgqJjJYp).

### Inferences
- For a non-salesy founder, the most repeatable combination is a **Chrome extension or app embedded in a high-traffic platform, plus a programmatic or free-tool SEO site** that feeds it. This combination scales internationally without a personal audience. X is powerful, but it depends on the founder's personal brand.
- Directories are cheap to submit to and worth doing for backlinks and long-tail discovery. They should not be the plan.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative comparison of channels across a large sample.
- I found no YouTube-led indie AI example with numbers.
- WordPress plugin AI examples were not covered within the search budget.

## Q3. Patterns among failures: AI wrappers commoditised by native features

### Takeaway
The best-documented "commoditised" case is a VC company, Jasper, not an indie. Most "wrapper extinction" statistics are unsourced blog claims. The concrete indie lesson comes from the other direction. Many survivors are **complements to the big chat apps rather than substitutes**: extensions that add folders, export and prompt queues to ChatGPT or Gemini, or tools that sit on X or Amazon. Being a complement carries its own platform risk.

### Cited Findings
- Jasper peaked at about $80M ARR, then growth stalled, followed by layoffs and a pivot to an "AI marketing platform". It was described as having no proprietary data or workflow lock-in. A claimed 70% revenue drop is unverified — [buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/failed-ai-startups-what-went-wrong-lessons-founders-2026); [Medium](https://medium.com/@aiempiremedia/why-openais-competitors-are-already-dead-and-don-t-know-it-yet-aebec3c926d1).
- Claims about failure counts are poorly sourced and inconsistent: "Custom GPTs killed dozens of chatbot wrappers"; "200+ funded wrappers cannibalised"; "3,800 shut down in 2025"; "65% churn within 90 days"; "80% projected to fail". None could be verified — [machinebrief](https://www.machinebrief.com/news/death-of-ai-wrapper-startups-wont-survive-2026); [valueaddvc](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/how-to-build-a-startup-in-a-market-where-ai-will-eventually-do-what-you-do); [roborhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/openai-killed-automation-startups/).
- Installs without monetisation: 20K users and $60 MRR (TimeYourWeb) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-a-chrome-extension-in-2-weeks-10-years-later-20-000-users-10-paying-customers-60-mrr-YaK5THoaKqXgTlgBjHgO).
- Product Hunt spikes that never convert (see Q2) — [DEV](https://dev.to/startupcafe/product-hunt-launches-look-impressive-but-do-they-bring-paying-customers-239l).

### Inferences
- Generic "text in, text out" tools are the most exposed: writing, summarising, chat-with-PDF, generic chatbots. Products like Easy Folders or Superpower ChatGPT survive by fixing UX gaps in ChatGPT, but the host can close those gaps at any time. This is a known and real risk.
- CheatMate-style products (exam "help") carry policy, ethics and Chrome Web Store takedown risk, on top of commoditisation.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous dataset of indie AI products that died after a specific OpenAI, Google or Anthropic feature launch.

## Q4. What makes a self-serve AI product defensible

### Takeaway
The examples point to three moats a solo founder can realistically build:
1. **Platform embedding / workflow position.** The tool lives where the user already works: the ChatGPT, X or Amazon Seller UI, or the Shopify admin.
2. **Vertical data or output specialisation.** Amazon sales estimates (Launch Fast), resume formatting with ATS knowledge (Rezi), trained headshot pipelines (HeadshotPro, Photo AI).
3. **Distribution assets the model vendor won't build.** SEO free-tool networks, newsletters, X audiences.

### Cited Findings
- Jasper's failure is attributed to having no proprietary data and no workflow lock-in — [buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/failed-ai-startups-what-went-wrong-lessons-founders-2026).
- Launch Fast combines product research, revenue estimates, keyword analysis and supplier discovery for Amazon sellers. This is vertical data plus a workflow position — [stealwhatworks](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/chrome-extensions-over-10k-month-now).
- SiteGPT's free-tool network is a compounding SEO asset — [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/sitegpt). Superpower ChatGPT's ~350K-subscriber newsletter is a distribution asset in its own right — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/building-a-free-chrome-extension-in-3-days-and-turning-it-into-a-5-figure-mrr-ecosystem-3rIbjigZxiFsrgqJjJYp).

### Inferences
- The most defensible pattern for a non-salesy solo founder appears to be a **vertical "agent-in-the-tab"**: an extension or marketplace app that automates a specific, repeated task for a niche professional (sellers, recruiters, creators, real-estate agents), with SEO free tools as top-of-funnel. Generic assistants and wrappers on the big LLM front-ends are the least defensible.

### Gaps
- There is no quantitative evidence on churn or retention by moat type for indie products.

## Q5. Payment rails that pay out to a Bangladesh resident

### Takeaway
**Stripe (and therefore ExtensionPay, which is built on Stripe) is not directly available to Bangladesh-registered businesses.** The usual workaround is a US LLC.

The most important late-2026 news is that **Lemon Squeezy is shutting down.** New sign-ups closed on Nov 16, 2026, and processing stops on Mar 31, 2027. Its Stripe Managed Payments successor covers only 37 locations, which likely excludes Bangladesh.

**Dodo Payments reportedly stopped onboarding new Bangladesh sellers from March 2026**, though its docs still list BD as a country it accepts payments from.

That leaves **Paddle (software only, payouts by wire or Payoneer)** and **Polar (single third-party claim; payouts via Stripe Connect Express, which is doubtful for BD)** as the main merchant-of-record candidates. All of these need direct confirmation with the provider.

### Cited Findings
- **Chrome Web Store** has had no native payments since 2021, so developers use external providers such as ExtensionPay, Paddle, Polar and Lemon Squeezy — [ExtensionPay](https://extensionpay.com/articles/extensionpay-is-the-chrome-web-store-payments-replacement); [microgaps](https://www.microgaps.com/gaps/chrome-extension-monetization-sdk-gap).
- **ExtensionPay:**
  - Integrates with Stripe, so sign-up works only where Stripe is supported. The 46-country figure comes from a roughly 2-year-old forum comment.
  - Fees: 5% per transaction plus Stripe fees in one source; another says 2% above $1,500/month (conflict) — [ExtensionPay](https://extensionpay.com/); [Kelviq](https://www.kelviq.com/blog/chrome-extension-payment-platforms/).
  - **Implication: not usable from Bangladesh without a Stripe-eligible entity, such as a US LLC.**
- **Stripe:** Not available to businesses registered in Bangladesh. A US LLC can apply for a US Stripe account, but there is no published rule on Bangladesh-resident owners, and some founders report declines from Relay and Airwallex — [globalsolo](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/us-bank-account-bangladeshi-founder-getting-paid-2026); [micahguru](https://micahguru.com/guides/best-payment-gateways-for-non-residents).
- **Dodo Payments:**
  - Official docs list Bangladesh among the 226 countries it accepts payments **from** (customers) — [Dodo docs](https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/list-of-countries-we-accept-payments-from).
  - Third-party trackers say Dodo **closed new seller onboarding in Bangladesh from March 2026** — [agentaos](https://agentaos.ai/answers/which-merchant-of-record-pays-out-to-my-country); [getpaidatlas](https://getpaidatlas.com/provider/dodo).
  - A Google Play review from a Bangladeshi user reports "We currently do not support your country" — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dodopayments.app&hl=en).
  - Fees are about 4% + 40¢ — [agentaos](https://agentaos.ai/answers/dodo-payments-fees).
  - **Status: likely unavailable for new BD sellers. Verify with Dodo.**
- **Paddle:**
  - Bangladesh is not on the reviewed exclusion list. It accepts software sellers only (no services). Fees are 5% + 50¢.
  - Payouts go by wire or Payoneer. Payout schedule conflicts: monthly once the balance passes $100 in one source, weekly rolling in another.
  - One source warns that Paddle's historical BD payout listing should not be relied on for new accounts — [globalsolo](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-2026); [agentaos Paddle fees](https://agentaos.ai/answers/paddle-fees); [agentaos](https://agentaos.ai/answers/which-merchant-of-record-pays-out-to-my-country).
- **Polar:**
  - An October 2026 third-party check marks Bangladesh as supported. But Polar pays out via Stripe Connect Express, which does not cover every country, so there is tension with Stripe's BD unavailability.
  - Withdrawals are possible from a $10 balance, with a 7-day hold for new accounts. Payout fees are reported as $2/month + 0.25% + 25¢ per payout.
  - No official Polar 2026 country list was confirmed — [agentaos](https://agentaos.ai/answers/which-merchant-of-record-pays-out-to-my-country); [buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/lemon-squeezy-vs-polar-paddle-merchant-of-record-2026); [paritydeals](https://www.paritydeals.com/polar-vs-lemon-squeezy-fees/).
- **Lemon Squeezy:**
  - Its docs historically listed Bangladesh for bank payouts — [LS docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries).
  - **But Lemon Squeezy is winding down:** new sign-ups closed Nov 16, 2026, migration tooling opens Dec 2, 2026, and processing stops Mar 31, 2027 — [LS migration docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/migration); [techstartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/11/16/stripe-shuts-down-lemon-squeezy-just-two-years-after-acquisition-giving-businesses-until-march-2027-to-migrate/).
  - Stripe Managed Payments accepts businesses in only 37 locations. About 82 LS payout countries have no in-Stripe path, and non-eligible sellers are pointed to Paddle, Polar or Dodo — [notevibes](https://notevibes.com/blog/lemon-squeezy-shutdown-where-to-move); [turbostarter](https://www.turbostarter.dev/blog/lemon-squeezy-vs-stripe).
  - **Not an option for a new BD seller.**
- **Payoneer** is widely used in Bangladesh as a payout or receiving rail, but it cannot take card payments from customers — [globalsolo](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-2026).
- **Marketplace-native billing:** Shopify App Store billing pays developers directly, with 0% revenue share on the first $1M from 2025 — [adsx](https://adsx.com/blog/how-much-shopify-app-developers-make). I did not verify Shopify Partner payout support for Bangladesh in this research.

### Inferences
- The practical stack for a Bangladesh resident selling a Chrome extension or SaaS internationally in late 2026:
  1. **Paddle**, as merchant of record, paying out to Payoneer or a BD bank via wire. Apply early, because approval is manual and software-only.
  2. **Polar**, as a candidate to test with a small first payout.
  3. A **US LLC plus Stripe (enabling ExtensionPay or Stripe Managed Payments)** as the scalable fallback, at the cost of formation and compliance overhead.
- Marketplaces with built-in billing (Shopify App Store, possibly others) reduce the payment-rail problem, provided their partner payouts reach BD (unverified).
- Avoid building on Lemon Squeezy now. Treat Dodo as unavailable unless Dodo support confirms otherwise in writing.

### Gaps
- Official current Paddle and Polar seller and payout country lists could not be fetched (docs.polar.sh was blocked).
- Shopify Partner, Atlassian Marketplace and WordPress.org payout support for Bangladesh was not verified.
- Bangladesh Bank rules on inward software-export remittance and tax treatment were not researched.
- ExtensionPay could support non-Stripe providers in future; I found no evidence that it does today.
